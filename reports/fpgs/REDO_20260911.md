## Runtime change in this branch

`FEATHER_PGS_SIMPLE_WORLD_ZERO=1` enables an **experimental, opt-in** pre-row
classifier for the original Kuka23+free6 matrix-free recipe. It checks the
current predicted velocity against all applicable raw contact normals,
activated position limits and rigid speed caps. An admitted world needs no
constraint impulse, so its contact/limit allocators publish zero row counts
and fresh negative contact routes. Every other world retains the original
complete solver. Original force conversion, dynamics and integration remain.

This does not delete collision geometry, lower iteration counts, reuse stale
forces, or replace the incoming predictor. Unsupported configurations use the
original path. The selector is not a public stable API or a general solver for
all task recipes. The default is disabled.

The ordinary FP32 rejection margins are conservative fallback decisions, not
an interval-proof requirement and not permission to violate constraints.
Original restitution incident velocity, prescribed motion and current MF
versus held dense response conventions are retained.

## What the earlier “losses” actually mean

- The 66 saved warm-collision examples were geometric patch-preservation
  failures, **not 66 demonstrated physical/task failures**. The previous gate
  also incorrectly rejected unchanged baseline queries under original MPR
  semantics; its producer and depth rules have been corrected.
- A widest saved5→1 change collapses an81.7mm patch. At a hypothetical fixed1N
  normal load, the maximum unavailable normal moment is0.06585N·m. That is a
  real change in conditional support capacity, but every selected gap is
  positive and actual solved loads were not saved. It does not establish
  current harm or harmlessness. Positive gaps may still carry speculative
  impulses when approaching.
- One saved interior contact can be removed without changing its static
  normal-moment hull. That alone does not prove dynamic equivalence under
  differing gaps, compliance, friction and finite iteration.
- The row-free zero path has direct bounded physical evidence:1742/2048
  captured world-states pass independent FP64 raw-normal/limit checks, require
  no velocity correction, and publish no rows. Actual original force conversion
  passes stable-graph nonzero→zero→nonzero transitions and poisoned unread-row
  controls on both GPUs. This establishes useful avoidable work in those states.

Acceptance should concern numerical convergence, force/momentum consistency
and task-relevant behavior, not bit identity or unconditional contact count.
Same-input full/full/warm force and residual diagnostics now complete on both
GPUs: three adjacent states, 512 worlds each, original native24 tiers and
generic12 fallback, unchanged substeps. The original full rollout remains the
trajectory; private response replays do not advance it. Source, private-storage,
operation-sequence and unchanged-live-state checks pass. This is a response
comparison, not a long-horizon task-success result.

Crucially, **all295 reduced-patch observations** in these new tests carried zero
normal load in both full and warm solves. All1000 original contact points on
those patches had zero solved normal impulse and remained feasible under the
original normal law at the warm velocity; the smallest residual was0.09835m/s.
Thus these sampled reductions removed no used original normal support. This
supports skipping inactive contact work; it does not prove every old saved case
or every future contact reduction harmless.

The warm manifold has a localized response tail well above full/full variation.
Across these three states, maximum object angular-velocity component differences
are 0.4775 rad/s RTX and 0.09130 rad/s GB; linear components differ by at most
0.006557 m/s and 0.005894 m/s. Maximum per-body force differences are 0.9071 N
and 0.7952 N; torque differences about body origins are 0.03245 N·m and
0.03926 N·m. These are maxima across all changed geometry, not isolated effects
of deleting a particular point, and not demonstrated task failures. Original-law
residuals at the warm velocity must be compared with the full solve at the same
budget, rather than treating either finite iterate as exact ground truth.
The worst incremental original normal-constraint violation is0.02240m/s RTX
and0.008059m/s GB, versus full/full changes of order1e-7m/s. In every captured
frame, the worst added violation is on a loaded **same-count** pair, not on a
reduced patch. The RTX worst-response world has no reduced pair. These are real
localized response changes requiring diagnosis of geometry and convergence;
neither a universal point-count gate nor a claim of task failure follows.

## Why the theoretically sparse experiment was slow

The old private A prototype computed201 composite-inertia actions instead of29
and scanned2142 factor products although only747 sparse intersections were
nonzero. Correcting those two ownership/arithmetic mistakes produces identical
factors and predictors in full16K captures, with refresh times:

- RTX:4603.936→2485.280µs,1.852×.
- GB:3310.592→1489.376µs,2.223×.

Reuse regresses772.080→847.872µs RTX and506.304→512.480µs GB. This is a
component improvement over an earlier **slow prototype**, not a gain over
accepted whole physics. Corrected A alone still costs more than the original
full dynamics pipeline. Keep original canonical FK/mass/predictor/publication
providers while developing row-free consumers; do not require a slow A/C rewrite.

new inverse producer or runtime mass-action proof. The first GPU implementation
admitted only29 one-bound worlds: its relative equality check normalized by the
near-zero result and rejected all200 zero-bias proposals. CPU IEEE FMA replay

the implementation error rather than disproving the algorithm.

Changing only that equality scale to the magnitudes of the input velocity,
applied correction and bias raises actual GPU admission to222 one-bound worlds:
52/58 RTX and55/57 GB across refresh/reuse. Combined with the1742 zero worlds,
that is1964/2048 captured states (95.90%). All original contact/other-bound/cap

velocity error is5.949e-8, physical bound residual is−3.014e-8, and scaled
held-mass momentum defect is7.446e-8. These small residuals are reported as
ordinary FP32 numerical error, not hidden or treated as a reason to preserve
unnecessary solver work. Eager/actual graph, refresh/reuse and original zero-count
GS publication checks pass on both GPUs.

The corrected classifier plus proposal and publication costs74.85–74.91µs RTX
and70.98–72.18µs GB for these512-world snapshots. Existing dynamics/inverse,

one-bound prototype remains separate from this branch's zero-only runtime.
An independent old-versus-corrected GPU audit confirms that all29 formerly

The remaining mixed-world solver also uses overly coarse ownership: in four
